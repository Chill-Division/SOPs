## Introduction

These advertising SOPs have been written for a Cultivator. They provide guidance
around what you may / may not say / share. These are not intended to be an
exhaustive list, but should be thorough enough to give a licensed cultivator a
good idea around what is and is not acceptable. The goal should always be to err
on the side of caution.

Medsafe have stipulated they will always take an "education-first" approach rather
than an enforcement-first approach. However, that is a lot of goodwill that has
been afforded to the medicinal cannabis industry as a whole that you would be
wise not to abuse. If it's blatant, they are still within their rights to come down
on you like a hammer.

> **No AI use.** These SOPs were written by a human. The underlying framework
> for displaying and formatting these SOPs online has been done with AI, but not
> the content itself. We won't accept commits, contributions or suggestions from
> LLMs. There is a lot of nuance in all of these, and the last thing that we need
> is some AI suggesting cultivation facilities are run under GACP / GMP or other
> such mistakes, due to a lack of that context-specific awareness.


All terminology follows [RFC 2119](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc2119)
(MUST / SHOULD / MAY).

## 1. What constitutes prohibited advertising

Rather than a list of do / don't, it's simpler to provide a list of common
activities that you should not be doing.

The reason all of this exists is that even a fully NZMQS-verified product is
still classed as an "Unapproved Medicine" under the Medicines Act 1981, and
unapproved medicines cannot be advertised. Not publicly, not privately, not by
word of mouth. Medsafe consider newspapers, brochures, websites, social media
(including re-posts / re-shares), TV, radio, presentations, price lists, stock
availability and testimonials to all be advertising, just to name a few. Under
§20.2(c) of the Act that can mean a 6 month prison term or a $20,000 fine for
an individual, or a $100,000 fine for a company. It applies to everyone, not
just licence holders.

1. Identifiable verified product
   1. This includes packaging, additional designs, promo materials

2. Sharing reviews / testimonials
   1. This includes quoting 3rd-party sources for reviews
   2. First-hand or 3rd-party, it doesn't matter, the restrictions capture
      them all

3. Sharing information to closed Facebook groups etc
   1. Advertising covers both public and private communications, so a
      "members only" group does not exempt you

4. TV / Radio / billboards that show / mentioning a product or where to get it

5. Publishing availability, or future availability. A "coming soon" post is
   still advertising

6. Publishing pricing information or price lists
   1. Supplying a price list unsolicited, or putting one up on a website, is
      considered advertising availability. This captures individuals as well
      as companies, right down to contributors to a community spreadsheet

7. Making any medical or therapeutic claims, or implying benefits
   1. This captures things as innocuous-sounding as "cannabis may help with
      insomnia", and even terpene claims such as "Linalool, found in Lavender,
      may promote relaxation". Lavender is an unapproved medicine too, the
      moment it's pitched therapeutically

8. Sending unsolicited emails, calls or brochures to doctors, clinics or the
   general public
   1. This includes general educational resources, and even international
      peer-reviewed research. If they didn't ask for it directly, you can't
      send it
   2. Even stating "Contact us for more information" may be in breach

9. Putting product representatives out on the road to "educate" doctors.
   Personal representation is advertising

10. Providing samples of any kind, to doctors, clinics, patients or the public
    (and definitely not staff)

11. Running a compassionate supply program. The law has no provision for it
    with unapproved medicines

12. Implying that a product cannot harm a person, or that it is not
    habit-forming. This includes devices such as vapes

13. Repeating unsubstantiated claims that other individuals or companies have
    made, including re-sharing their posts

## 2. What you can still do

It's not all doom and gloom. Despite the restrictions there is still a vast
area you can operate in, and plenty of it makes for better content anyway.

1. Show what goes on behind the scenes. Cultivation, the build / fit-out of
   the facility, the licensing journey, strain names, lineage and genetics
   details are all fine

2. Show the manufacturing process, so long as no final product is named or
   shown
   1. "We're extracting cannabis to put into tablets to get out to patients"
      is fine. "This is going into our Cannabis Oil 9000" is not
   2. Showing final / packed products or doing unboxings is not permitted

3. Make broad, non-descriptive company statements such as "we grow and
   manufacture medicinal cannabis products"

4. Run "public good" advertising that doesn't mention a product, availability,
   or solicit anyone
   1. Have it pre-vetted through a Therapeutic Advertising Pre-vetting Service
      (TAPS by ANZA) before running it

5. Sell or give away merch, so long as it doesn't mention or hint at any
   product. The company name is fine, "Our White Rhino flower" is not

6. Notify an opted-in mailing list about a newly verified product, without
   advertising availability such as where to buy it

7. Answer questions that a healthcare professional or patient has directly
   asked you. Only privately, and only because they asked

## 3. Example advertisements

<!-- Copy any block below (heading, explainer, blockquote) as the template
     for each new example. -->

### "Northern Lights, coming soon to ClinicXYZ"

Posting this on social media / print / web etc is strictly prohibited.

> **This is advertising availability:** Medsafe would deem this as inappropriate
> and you would likely receive a breach notice.
>
> **More suitable:** Nothing, this is just a hard pass. Don't mention the
> product name, and don't mention the clinic it's available from.

### "Dank Bud 25%, $420 / oz at PharmacyXYZ"

Publishing pricing anywhere public, be it your website, a flyer, or contributing
to one of the community spreadsheets floating around Reddit.

> **This is advertising availability and pricing:** Price lists are captured by
> the restrictions even though we'd consider them a major public good, and they
> capture individuals just as much as licensed companies and pharmacies.
>
> **More suitable:** Nothing publicly. A pharmacy can tell a patient the price
> when that patient asks them directly.

### "Jason takes Flower20 for his back pain"

A testimonial, whether it's a post of your own, a quote in a brochure, or
re-sharing something a patient posted about you.

> **This is a testimonial and a therapeutic claim:** No medical or therapeutic
> claims may be made about an unapproved medicine, and first-hand or 3rd-party
> makes no difference.
>
> **More suitable:** Nothing from you. Interestingly, a GP could say "I've had
> people come to me for back pain, and treated it", because no product and no
> cannabis is mentioned. You don't have that luxury.

### "Linalool, found in Lavender, may promote relaxation and sleep"

The classic educational terpene infographic, destined for social media.

> **This is a therapeutic claim:** It reads like harmless education, but the
> moment a benefit is attached it becomes a claim about an unapproved medicine.
> Yes, even Lavender is one when it's pitched therapeutically.
>
> **More suitable:** Talk about terpene profiles factually. "This cultivar has a
> linalool-forward profile" describes the plant without promising anyone a good
> night's sleep.

### "All doctors in New Zealand can prescribe medicinal cannabis. Have you asked your GP if it's right for you?"

A "public good" billboard or print advertisement. We have literally run this
one.

> **This one is fine:** No product, no names, no availability, no soliciting.
> Advertising as a public good without mentioning a product falls within the
> restrictions, and an independent pre-vetting service even approved the flower
> in the background of ours.
>
> **Even more suitable:** Run it through TAPS before it goes anywhere, like we
> did. Their pre-approval is cheap insurance.

### "Yay, our Products X and Y are now NZMQS verified!"

The celebratory new product announcement, on your socials or your own website.

> **This is technically prohibited:** Companies aren't permitted to announce
> newly approved products. In practice Medsafe haven't (yet) pursued anyone for
> a congratulatory post with no availability attached, but in reality they could
> if they wanted to.
>
> **More suitable:** A broad statement such as "we grow and manufacture
> medicinal cannabis products", or notify your opted-in mailing list without
> mentioning where to buy.
